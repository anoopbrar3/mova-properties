Why you can trust it

Four rules, enforced in code.

Not policy, not a disclaimer at the bottom of a report. These are build constraints — if the software breaks one, the build fails.

Retrieval, never training

AeroBee is not fine-tuned on your data. Answers are retrieved from your approved sources and grounded in them. Training on records would destroy the traceability that makes an answer usable at all.

The citation verifier

Every claim is extracted after generation and matched to a source. Unmatched claims are stripped, and the count appears on the answer — including when it isn't zero.

Refusal is a real answer

"No supported answer" ships as a first-class result. Open the last tab in the demo above — the question AeroBee declines, and the three claims it discarded to get there.

Advisory, and read-only

AeroBee locates evidence and flags where it's missing. It never determines compliance, minimum-equipment applicability or airworthiness, and it writes nothing back to your systems.

Why this matters now

Europe's proposed aviation-AI framework asks maintenance organisations to demonstrate that an AI's output was grounded in approved data rather than generated. That requirement is where AeroBee started — not something added once the rules appeared.

Your data

The questions procurement asks first.

Answered here rather than in a follow-up call, because these are the right questions and vague answers should worry you.

Do you train on our data?

No. Not on records, not on flight data, not on manuals. Retrieval and grounding only. This isn't a policy position we could quietly change — training would break the citation trail the product is built on.

Can AeroBee change anything?

No. Every connector is read-only. There is no write path to a maintenance system, technical log or record of any kind. The software can read your systems and cannot alter them.

Where does the data live?

Tenant-isolated, encrypted in transit and at rest, with a Canadian residency option and private deployment available for operators whose contracts require it.

Who sees flight data?

Flight data is de-identified before analysis, and crew identity is separated from it, consistent with non-punitive programme requirements. Access is role-based and logged.

What happens if we leave?

Your corpus is exported and deleted on request. AeroBee retains no derived index of your data, and no cross-operator pattern sharing happens without a separate, explicit agreement.

Is this a certified system?

No, and it doesn't claim to be. AeroBee is ground-based advisory software. It is not installed on aircraft, is not part of any certified system, and makes no airworthiness determinations.
